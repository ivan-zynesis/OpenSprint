# DEC-042: The Three Review Questions Live on the Initiative

## Question

[[DS-ATTEST-AS-REVIEW]] makes every attestation answer reversibility, blast radius and RACI. Those
are properties of a **change**, not of a measure — so they do not belong in a record's
`## Measures`. Where do they go, and in what shape?

## Decision

An `## Attestation` section on the **initiative**, written by `/opsp:attest`:

```markdown
## Attestation

- **door**: two-way — the feature flag reverts without data migration
- **blast-radius**: checkout path, payment reconciliation, the nightly settlement job

### RACI

| Concern | Handled by | R | A | C | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| settlement mismatch alerting | system — pagerduty svc-settle | — | — | — | — |
| manual reconciliation if the job fails | **not automated** | ops-oncall | head-of-eng | finance | support |
```

Three rules:

**`door` is `one-way` or `two-way`**, by explicit lookup. Anything else is reported.

**A concern handled by a system names the system and leaves RACI empty.** RACI is the **residue
after automation** — it grows only where the automation does not.

**A concern not handled by a system must name at least R and A.** C and I are optional.

New loops: `reversibility-unstated`, `blast-radius-unstated`, `raci-unassigned`.

## Consideration Factors

| Factor | Weight | On the initiative | Per measure | A separate record type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matches what it describes | HIGH | A change is the unit ✓ | A measure is not a change ✗ | ✓ |
| Harvestable | HIGH | Initiatives are parsed ✓ | ✓ | New parser ✗ |
| Survives the merge | HIGH | Travels with the branch ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Reads where you'd look | HIGH | Beside the milestones ✓ | Scattered across records ✗ | Another file to find ✗ |
| Duplication across measures | MED | None ✓ | Repeated per bar ✗ | None ✓ |

## Rationale

Reversibility and blast radius are properties of **the whole change**, not of any one bar — a
one-way door is one-way whichever measure you are looking at. Putting them per-measure would repeat
the same answer and invite three measures to disagree about it.

RACI belongs with them for the same reason, and because its subject is what happens *after* the
merge. The initiative is the only artifact that spans from proposal to production.

**The system-or-human split is what keeps the table small.** Naming the system and leaving RACI
empty is a complete answer — and it makes the automation gap literally visible as a column of named
humans. A team that automates more watches the table shrink, which is the right incentive.

Requiring R and A but not C and I reflects what is actually load-bearing: someone does the work,
someone answers for it. Consulted and informed are useful and rarely the reason an incident goes
unhandled.

## Invalidation Trigger

If initiatives routinely list a concern as handled by a system that then fails without anyone
noticing, "handled by a system" is being used to avoid naming a human, and the rule needs a named
owner even for automated concerns. If the RACI table regularly exceeds a handful of rows, the
initiative is too large rather than the table too detailed.
