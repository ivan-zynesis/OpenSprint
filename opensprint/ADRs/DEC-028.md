# DEC-028: A Decision Is Owned by `dev` or `devops`, Carries No Role, and May CC Other Hats

## Question

[[DEC-016]] lets a record declare several hats. Applied to decisions, that lets an ADR route to `maintainer` — which declares roles — while no ADR in this repository has ever carried one. Writing `DEC-020`, `DEC-021` and `DEC-023` harvested three immediate `record-unclassified` loops. Do decisions take roles, and which hats may own one?

## Decision

Three rules.

**1. A decision is owned by `dev` or `devops`, or both.** Never by `product` or `maintainer`. A concern that genuinely belongs to those hats is a driver-spec, not a decision.

**2. Other hats on a decision are CC, not ownership.** A decision may list `maintainer` or `product` to say "this affects you" — it does not transfer accountability, and the review, escalation and triage owner remains the dev/devops owner.

**3. A decision carries no `role`.** Role classification applies to driver-specs only, and `record-unclassified` must never fire on a decision regardless of which hats it declares.

Frontmatter is unchanged; `hats` already accepts a list. The owner is the `dev`/`devops` member of that list.

## Consideration Factors

| Factor | Weight | Owner + CC, no role | Roles for decisions | Single-hat decisions only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One accountable owner | HIGH | Owner is always dev/devops ✓ | Orthogonal to the problem — | Owner is unambiguous ✓ |
| Cross-cutting decisions stay visible | HIGH | CC keeps them in both views ✓ | — | Affected hat never sees it ✗ |
| Fits how dev/devops actually work | HIGH | No OGSM to classify against ✓ | Invents a vocabulary nobody needs ✗ | ✓ |
| Stops the false `record-unclassified` | HIGH | Exempt by rule ✓ | Only by inventing roles ✗ | Only by banning the hat ✗ |
| Preserves [[DEC-016]] | MED | Multi-hat retained ✓ | ✓ | Reverts it ✗ |

## Rationale

`product` and `maintainer` run OGSM; `dev` and `devops` deliberately do not. Their domain is generic good practice and industry standard — knowledge that is general rather than project-specific, and that a codebase should not be restating. There is nothing for a decision's role to classify it *against*, which is why no ADR in this repository has ever carried one, and why the empty `DEFAULT_ROLES` entries for `dev` and `devops` were right all along.

The ownership rule follows from the same place. When something concerns product or maintainer, it is almost always a *constraint* rather than a *choice* — which makes it a driver-spec. A decision is how dev or devops answers such a constraint, so its owner is whoever will build and maintain the answer. CC exists because a decision frequently matters to the hat whose constraint it answers, and [[DS-SME-OWNERSHIP]] requires the hat file to have one accountable owner rather than two.

The third rule also protects how decisions are compiled. [[DS-BIG-PICTURE]] requires a view to select rather than enumerate, and a decision routinely touches several sections at once — runtime topology, entity schema and deployment in a single record. Compaction has to read decisions as a **big picture** and merge them into a description, which is exactly what `DEFAULT_SECTIONS_BY_HAT.dev` already does by taking `inputs: 'decisions'` with no role filter. A role on a decision would invite the opposite: filing each one under a single section and enumerating.

## Invalidation Trigger

If a decision genuinely cannot be owned by `dev` or `devops`, the record is a constraint wearing a decision's clothes and belongs in `driver-specs/`. If `dev` or `devops` ever adopts a classification framework of its own, rule 3 is reopened — but not before.
