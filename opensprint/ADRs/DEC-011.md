# DEC-011: Recompile Stale Sections From Full Inputs, Never Summary-Plus-Delta

## Question

When records change, should `compact` update the affected view sections incrementally from the previous summary plus the delta, or re-render them from the complete input set?

## Decision

The delta decides *which* sections are out of date. Each such section is then re-rendered from its **full** input set. The previous rendering is never an input to the new one.

## Consideration Factors

| Factor | Weight | Rebuild from full inputs | Summary plus delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fidelity over time | HIGH | Each render is one hop from the record ✓ | Compounding loss; after many rounds nobody knows what it derives from ✗ |
| Cost | MED | Re-reads the section's records ✗ | Cheaper per update ✓ |
| Auditability | HIGH | The manifest's input list is the complete provenance ✓ | Provenance is a chain of renderings ✗ |

## Rationale

This is the same reasoning as building an image once and promoting it by digest: the artifact that gets trusted must be traceable to its source in one hop, not through a chain of transformations each of which could have lost something.

A partial supersession makes the risk concrete. Folding `DEC-064` into a summary already built from `DEC-062` requires the compiler to understand what the earlier summary asserted and which part is now wrong. Re-rendering from both records requires only that it read both.

Propagation across the decision tree is deliberately *not* handled by re-rendering. A superseded ancestor does not trigger re-rendering of its descendants; those are flagged as open loops pointing at `/opsp:rebuild-assess`, whose job this already is.

## Invalidation Trigger

If full re-rendering becomes too slow for large surrogates, the fix is narrower section boundaries — more sections, each with fewer inputs — not incremental summarisation.
