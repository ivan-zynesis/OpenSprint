# DEC-008: The Charter Compiles From Governance Records Too

## Question

A hat's charter states what it owns, what it may not trade away, and when it escalates. If [[DEC-006]] makes the whole view non-editable, where does the charter come from?

## Decision

The charter compiles too, from governance records — driver-specs and ADRs that describe the operating model rather than the system's behaviour. To change what a hat owns, record a decision.

A project with no governance records gets a thin charter, which is the honest report: ownership has not been decided yet.

## Consideration Factors

| Factor | Weight | Charter compiled | Charter hand-authored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consistency with [[DEC-006]] | HIGH | One rule for the whole file ✓ | A hand-edited region inside a derived file ✗ |
| Source material exists | HIGH | cashier's `DEC-054` and `DS-CHANGE-GOVERNANCE`, pixlr-web's `DS-BRANCH-DISCIPLINE` are charter content already in the record ✓ | Would duplicate them ✗ |
| Ownership becomes reviewable | MED | A decision with rationale and an invalidation trigger ✓ | A convention nobody reviewed ✗ |
| Cold-start quality | MED | Thin until governance is recorded ✗ | Can be written immediately ✓ |

## Rationale

Ownership boundaries are exactly the kind of thing that should be argued once and recorded, not asserted in a document header. Cashier's CODEOWNERS is derived from the blast radius DECISION-MAP.md already computes — that is a charter, recorded as a decision, and it is reviewable because of it.

The cold-start weakness is acceptable under [[DEC-006]]'s logic: a thin charter accurately reports that nobody has decided, which is a backlog item rather than a defect in the compiler.

## Invalidation Trigger

If projects consistently produce empty charters because operating-model constraints are never recorded, the right response is a prompt in propose to capture them — not an authored region in the view.
