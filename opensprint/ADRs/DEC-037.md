# DEC-037: A Strategy Cell Renders SWOT-Like, Compiled From the Decisions It Cites

## Question

[[DS-MAINTAINER-INHERITED]] gives each objective a Strategy cell, and [[DEC-020]] says that cell cites its dev/devops implementers rather than describing them. What shape does the cell itself take?

## Decision

**SWOT-like, deliberately not SWOT**, with four parts per choice:

| Part | Means here |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | why this choice |
| **Weakness** | the trade-off it carries |
| **Opportunity** | **the alternatives corresponding to the choice made** |
| **Threat** | the risk, and what would mitigate it |

`Opportunity` is a **deliberate redefinition**. Standard SWOT means external upside; here it means the alternatives weighed against the choice. Documented as a redefinition so it does not read as a misunderstanding.

The cell is **compiled from the ADRs it cites**, not authored:

| SWOT part | Source in the ADR |
|---|---|
| Strength | the ✓ cells of the chosen column |
| Weakness | the ✗ cells of the chosen column |
| Opportunity | the other columns of the consideration table |
| Threat | `## Invalidation Trigger` |

## Consideration Factors

| Factor | Weight | SWOT-like, compiled | Free prose | Authored separately |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing new to write | HIGH | Already in every ADR ✓ | ✓ | A second effort ✗ |
| Cannot drift from the decisions | HIGH | Derived ✓ | Drifts ✗ | Drifts ✗ |
| Trade-offs are visible | HIGH | Weakness is a required part ✓ | Easy to omit ✗ | ✓ |
| Readable by senior management | HIGH | Four short parts ✓ | Varies ✗ | ✓ |
| Fits a one-line strategy | LOW | Four parts may be heavy ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |

## Rationale

The structure already exists. Every ADR in this repository carries a weighted consideration table and an invalidation trigger, which is SWOT in long form — so the strategy cell is a **condensation of material already written**, satisfying [[DS-DOCUMENTATION-AS-OUTPUT]] without a second effort.

This closes the loop with [[DEC-020]] neatly. The maintainer strategy does not describe the mechanism; it renders the SWOT compiled from the dev/devops decisions it cites. The altitude rule and the cell shape turn out to be the same arrangement seen from two sides.

Requiring Weakness as a named part is the quiet value. A strategy stated without its trade-off reads as free, and the next person to revisit it has to rediscover what it cost — which is the same failure [[DEC-019]]'s `bar-without-neighbour` catches one level down.

The cost: four parts is heavy for a strategy that is genuinely one sentence. Parts with nothing to say are omitted rather than padded, exactly as an empty objective row is left empty.

## Invalidation Trigger

If Opportunity is routinely empty, decisions are being made without alternatives considered — a finding about the decisions, not about this shape. If the redefinition of Opportunity keeps confusing readers, rename that part rather than abandoning the framework.
