# DEC-007: Open Loops Are the Backlog; Explore to Propose Is the Triage

## Question

`compact` can detect open loops — a constraint with no decision, a decision with no guard, a rule guarding a superseded decision. What happens to them once detected?

## Decision

Open loops are the backlog. `/opsp:explore` followed by `/opsp:propose` is the triage that turns them into initiatives. This is what an agile squad does as backlog triage, expressed in workflows that already exist.

No new mechanism is introduced to act on an open loop. `compact` reports; the operator or a hat owner triages through explore; propose classifies the outcome back into driver-specs, ADRs and milestones; apply executes; the record changes; compact recompiles.

## Consideration Factors

| Factor | Weight | explore → propose | A dedicated remediation workflow |
|---|---|---|---|
| New machinery | HIGH | None — both workflows exist ✓ | A further workflow to build and maintain ✗ |
| Judgement applied | HIGH | A human decides which gaps matter ✓ | Risks auto-filing work nobody chose ✗ |
| Fits the loop | HIGH | Closes [[DS-LOOP-CLOSURE]]'s last arrow ✓ | Same, but redundantly ✗ |

## Rationale

The facilitation role [[DS-LOOP-CLOSURE]] describes has two halves: *noticing*, and *deciding what to do about it*. Only the first can be encoded. compact does the noticing; the second half is triage, and triage is judgement.

This is what makes rule harvesting worth building in v1: its output is not a report, it is backlog generation. That is why [[DEC-013]] is in scope rather than deferred.

## Invalidation Trigger

If the open-loop list grows large enough that triaging it through conversation becomes impractical, a ranked or filtered presentation may be needed — but the triage itself should stay in explore, not move into compact.
